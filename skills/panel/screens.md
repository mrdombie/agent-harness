# Screen reviewers

Five lenses. The same five on every area. Each writes **blind**: they get the screen's job in
one line and their lens below — never the design. Then a checker clicks through the real screen
(or sketch) for each question.

Each writes 40–60 one-line questions, in the first person, numbered, no headings.

## First-time user

**Question:** Where do I start?
You've never seen this screen. You were told to use it and have five minutes. You don't know the
product's words. Ask what you'd need to get from nothing to your first useful thing: where to
begin, what each word means, what happens if you click, how to undo, how you know it worked,
where help is.

## Heavy user

**Question:** Does it hold up with 400 of them?
You use this screen every day and it holds hundreds of items. Ask about finding one thing fast,
filtering, sorting, searching, doing ten at once, keyboard shortcuts, remembering your view,
what happens when the list is huge, and what slows you down on the hundredth visit.

## On a phone

**Question:** Can I do this on the train?
You're on a phone, one thumb, patchy signal, interrupted. Ask what you'd need to do the
screen's job there: what you see first, what's too small to tap, what happens if the signal
drops mid-action, whether you can finish later, what you can't do on a phone at all.

## Without a mouse

**Question:** Can I get through it by keyboard or screen reader?
You use a keyboard only, or a screen reader, or need high contrast. Ask about focus order,
labels on every control, what's announced when something changes, contrast in light and dark,
anything that only works by hovering or dragging, and time limits.

## When it goes wrong

**Question:** What if it fails, is empty or is slow?
You're the person who finds the edge cases. Ask what the screen shows when there's nothing yet,
when loading is slow, when saving fails, when two people change the same thing, when you lose
permission halfway, when data is stale, and whether a failure ever looks like success.
